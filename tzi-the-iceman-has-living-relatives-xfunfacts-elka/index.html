Ötzi the Iceman Has Living Relatives xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] DNA testing identified 19 living men in Austria who share genetic markers with Ötzi the Iceman. The famous mummy still has distant relatives alive today.

Ötzi the Iceman is one of the world's most famous archaeological discoveries. Found frozen in the Alps in 1991, his naturally preserved body dates back more than 5,000 years. In 2013, researchers compared his DNA with modern genetic samples and identified 19 living men in Austria who shared a rare genetic marker inherited through the paternal line. Although these individuals are not close relatives, they likely descend from a distant common ancestor who lived thousands of years ago. The discovery demonstrated how modern genetics can connect ancient human remains with living populations while providing valuable insights into prehistoric migration, ancestry, and population history across Europe. Ötzi continues to offer scientists remarkable information about ancient health, diet, technology, and daily life.

Did you know?

Researchers identified living Austrian men sharing a paternal genetic lineage with Ötzi, but sharing Y-chromosome markers does not establish direct descent from the 5,300-year-old individual.
