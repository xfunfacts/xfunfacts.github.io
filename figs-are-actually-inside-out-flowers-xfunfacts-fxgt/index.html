Figs Are Actually Inside-Out Flowers xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Figs are actually inside-out flowers with hidden blooms. Their unusual pollination depends on a remarkable partnership with tiny fig wasps.

Although most people think of figs as ordinary fruits, they are actually inverted flowers known as syconia. Instead of blooming outward like most flowers, a fig develops with its flowers hidden inside a hollow structure. Pollination is carried out by tiny fig wasps that enter through a small opening at the end of the fruit. Female wasps lay eggs inside male figs, while female figs trap the insects, allowing pollination to occur without producing offspring. Enzymes inside the fig eventually break down the trapped wasps, leaving no recognizable remains in the edible fruit. This remarkable relationship between figs and wasps has evolved over millions of years, with many fig species depending on a single wasp species for reproduction. It remains one of nature's most fascinating examples of coevolution between plants and insects.

Did you know?

A fig is a syconium, a hollow structure lined internally with many tiny flowers, so describing it as an “inside-out flower” is a useful analogy rather than a literal botanical definition.
