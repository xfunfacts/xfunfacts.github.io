Mozart Recreated Secret Vatican Music From Memory xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] At just 14 years old, Mozart heard a secret Vatican composition once and reproduced it entirely from memory.

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart displayed extraordinary musical talent from an early age, and one of the most famous stories about his genius involves a closely guarded Vatican composition. For nearly 150 years, Gregorio Allegri's sacred work Miserere was considered so exclusive that it was performed only twice each year inside the Sistine Chapel, and copying the music was strictly forbidden. At just fourteen years old, Mozart attended one of the performances during a visit to Rome. According to historical accounts, he returned home afterward and transcribed the entire composition from memory with astonishing accuracy, making only minor corrections after hearing it a second time. The achievement amazed musicians and church officials alike, ultimately contributing to wider publication of the once-secret masterpiece. Whether every detail of the story is perfectly accurate or not, it remains one of the most celebrated examples of Mozart's phenomenal musical memory and exceptional ear.

Did you know?

In 1770, the teenage Mozart transcribed Gregorio Allegri’s Miserere after hearing it in the Sistine Chapel, although the famous story that he reproduced it perfectly after only one hearing is probably embellished.
