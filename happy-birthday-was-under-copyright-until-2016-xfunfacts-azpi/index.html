Happy Birthday Was Under Copyright Until 2016 xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Singing Happy Birthday in a movie once came with a price tag. Until 2016, the famous tune was still protected by copyright.

For decades, singing "Happy Birthday to You" in a commercial setting could require paying licensing fees because the song was believed to be protected by copyright. Restaurants, television shows, films, and advertisers often avoided using the familiar tune or paid substantial fees to include it legally. The copyright was managed by Warner/Chappell Music, which collected millions of dollars in licensing revenue over the years. However, legal challenges eventually questioned whether the company actually owned the rights to the song's lyrics. In 2015, a U.S. federal court ruled that Warner/Chappell did not possess a valid copyright claim over the famous lyrics, leading to a settlement the following year. Since 2016, "Happy Birthday to You" has effectively entered the public domain, allowing people to perform and use it freely without paying royalties. The case became one of the most famous copyright disputes in music history, highlighting how intellectual property law can influence even the world's most familiar songs. Today, the birthday anthem is once again freely shared at celebrations around the globe.
