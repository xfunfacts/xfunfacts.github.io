The Oldest Unopened Wine Bottle Is Over 1,650 Years Old xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Hidden inside an ancient Roman tomb, a bottle of wine survived unopened for more than sixteen centuries.

The oldest known unopened bottle of wine offers a remarkable glimpse into life during the Roman Empire. Often called the Speyer wine bottle, it was discovered inside a Roman tomb in Germany and has remained sealed for more than 1,650 years. Archaeologists believe the bottle dates to the fourth century AD and was likely placed in the grave as part of ancient burial customs. Unlike most ancient wines that completely evaporated or deteriorated, this bottle still contains liquid because it was carefully sealed with wax and protected beneath a layer of olive oil that limited exposure to air. Although the contents have changed significantly over the centuries and are no longer suitable for drinking, the bottle remains an extraordinary archaeological artifact. Researchers continue studying it to better understand Roman winemaking techniques, burial practices, and methods of preserving valuable goods. The survival of both the bottle and its contents highlights the durability of ancient craftsmanship and provides an exceptionally rare connection to everyday life nearly two thousand years ago.

Did you know?

The Speyer wine bottle was discovered in a Roman tomb in Germany in 1867 and dates to approximately AD 325–350; its sealed contents have been preserved for centuries, although older liquid wine has since been identified in Spain.
