Triton Orbits Neptune Backward xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Triton circles Neptune in the opposite direction from almost every other large moon. Its unusual orbit hints at a dramatic past.

Triton, Neptune's largest moon, is unique because it orbits in the opposite direction of its planet's rotation, a motion known as a retrograde orbit. This unusual path suggests Triton did not form alongside Neptune but was likely captured from the distant Kuiper Belt long ago. Triton is also one of the coldest known worlds in the Solar System, with icy plains, frozen nitrogen, and even active geysers that release material from beneath its surface. Over billions of years, its orbit is slowly shrinking, and scientists predict Triton may eventually break apart under Neptune's gravity, potentially forming a spectacular ring system. Its unusual history makes Triton one of the most intriguing moons ever discovered.

Did you know?

Triton's retrograde orbit strongly suggests that Neptune captured it from the outer Solar System rather than forming it alongside the planet.
