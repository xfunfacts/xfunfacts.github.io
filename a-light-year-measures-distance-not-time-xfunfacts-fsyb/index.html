A Light-Year Measures Distance, Not Time xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] A light-year measures distance rather than time, representing how far light travels in one year through space.

A light-year is a unit of distance rather than time, representing how far light travels in one year through the vacuum of space. Since light moves at about 186,000 miles per second, one light-year equals approximately 5.88 trillion miles, or 9.46 trillion kilometers. Astronomers use light-years because ordinary units such as miles or kilometers become impractical when measuring the enormous distances between stars and galaxies. For example, the nearest star beyond the Sun is more than four light-years away. This measurement helps scientists describe the true scale of the universe.

Did you know?

One light-year is about 9.46 trillion kilometers, a unit astronomers use because ordinary distance units become impractical across interstellar space.
