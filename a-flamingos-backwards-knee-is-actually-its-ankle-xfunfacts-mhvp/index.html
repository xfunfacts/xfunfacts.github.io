A Flamingo's "Backwards Knee" Is Actually Its Ankle xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] What appears to be a flamingo's backward knee is actually its ankle, while the true knee is hidden higher up.

What looks like a flamingo's backward-bending knee is actually its ankle. The true knee is hidden much higher up inside the bird's body beneath its feathers, making the lower leg appear unusual to human observers. This leg structure helps flamingos wade efficiently through shallow water while searching for food. Combined with their long necks and specialized beaks, their unique anatomy makes them perfectly adapted for life in wetlands.

Did you know?

Flamingos are not naturally bright pink at hatching; their color develops from carotenoid pigments obtained through foods such as algae and crustaceans.
