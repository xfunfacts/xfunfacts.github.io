Maps Don't Always Have North at the Top xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Maps have not always placed north at the top. Many Indigenous Australian maps use stories, landmarks, or different orientations to represent geography.

Maps do not have to place north at the top. While modern maps often follow that convention, many cultures have developed their own ways of representing geography. Some Indigenous Australian maps emphasize storytelling, landmarks, travel routes, and relationships between places instead of compass directions. Others may place south at the top depending on cultural perspective or practical needs. This demonstrates that maps are not simply scientific tools but also cultural expressions that reflect how different societies understand and navigate the world.

Did you know?

Many medieval European maps placed east at the top, while traditional Islamic maps sometimes placed south upward, showing that north-up orientation is a convention rather than a geographic necessity.
