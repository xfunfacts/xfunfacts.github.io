Elephant Tusks Are Actually Teeth xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Elephant tusks are elongated incisor teeth that continue growing throughout an elephant's lifetime.

Elephant tusks are actually elongated incisor teeth that continue growing throughout the animal's life. Both African male and female elephants usually develop tusks, while in Asian elephants they are more common in males. Elephants use their tusks to dig for water, strip bark from trees, move heavy objects, defend themselves, and compete with rivals. Because tusks grow continuously, older elephants often have much larger ones than younger individuals. These remarkable teeth are among the most distinctive features of the world's largest land animals.

Did you know?

Tusks are used for digging, stripping bark, moving objects, and defense, and individual elephants may favor one tusk much like humans favor one hand.
