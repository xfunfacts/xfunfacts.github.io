The Average Bank Teller Loses $250 Each Year xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Bank tellers may average about $250 in annual cash shortages, though modern banking systems greatly reduce counting errors.

The average bank teller is often estimated to account for about $250 in cash shortages each year, although the exact figure varies by institution and reporting methods. Banks handle thousands of cash transactions daily, making occasional counting errors or minor discrepancies inevitable despite strict procedures. Modern banking systems use computerized tracking, surveillance cameras, and multiple verification steps to minimize losses and quickly identify mistakes. Most discrepancies are small and are resolved through routine balancing procedures rather than fraud investigations.

Did you know?

Banks reconcile tellers' cash drawers against transaction records to identify shortages and overages, but there is no reliable nationwide evidence establishing an average annual loss of exactly $250 per teller.
