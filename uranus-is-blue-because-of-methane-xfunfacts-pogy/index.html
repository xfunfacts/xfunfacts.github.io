Uranus Is Blue Because of Methane xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Uranus looks blue because of methane in its atmosphere. The gas filters sunlight to give the distant planet its distinctive color.

Uranus appears blue because methane gas in its atmosphere absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight while reflecting blue and green light back into space. This gives the planet its distinctive pale blue color when viewed through telescopes or spacecraft. Uranus is one of the Solar System's four giant outer planets and is classified as an ice giant because it contains large amounts of water, ammonia, and methane beneath its atmosphere. It is also unusual for rotating almost on its side, giving it one of the most extreme axial tilts of any planet. These unique characteristics make Uranus one of the most fascinating and mysterious worlds in our Solar System.

Did you know?

Methane absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight, but haze particles and other atmospheric effects also influence Uranus's pale blue-green appearance.
