Rainbows Are Seen When the Sun Is Low xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Rainbows appear when the Sun is low in the sky and sunlight is refracted, reflected, and dispersed by raindrops.

Rainbows are most easily seen when the Sun is low in the sky, usually during the morning or late afternoon, because sunlight must strike raindrops at the proper angle to produce the familiar arc of colors. As sunlight enters each raindrop, it slows, bends, reflects inside the droplet, and bends again as it exits. This process separates white sunlight into its different wavelengths, creating the visible spectrum from red to violet. Every observer sees a slightly different rainbow because the light reaching each person's eyes comes from different raindrops. Under ideal conditions, people may also see double rainbows, supernumerary bands, or even circular rainbows when viewed from aircraft or mountain peaks, making rainbows one of nature's most beautiful optical phenomena.

Did you know?

Rainbows are complete circles centered on the antisolar point, but from the ground the horizon usually hides the lower portion of the circle.
