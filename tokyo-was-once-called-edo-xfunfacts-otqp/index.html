Tokyo Was Once Called Edo xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Tokyo was originally called Edo before becoming Japan's capital and receiving its current name in 1868.

Japan's capital was known as Edo until 1868, when it was renamed Tokyo, meaning "Eastern Capital," after Emperor Meiji moved the imperial court there. During the Edo period, the city grew into one of the world's largest urban centers under the rule of the Tokugawa shogunate. The transformation into Tokyo marked the beginning of Japan's rapid modernization during the Meiji Restoration. Today, Tokyo is one of the world's leading centers of finance, technology, culture, and transportation while still preserving many historic temples, gardens, and traditional neighborhoods.

Did you know?

Edo became the political center of Japan under the Tokugawa shogunate in 1603, long before Emperor Meiji renamed it Tokyo, meaning “Eastern Capital,” in 1868.
