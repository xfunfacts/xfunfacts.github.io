Why the Word “Barbarian” Was Originally an Insult xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The word "barbarian" originally referred to people who did not speak Greek. Ancient Greeks thought foreign languages sounded like repetitive "bar bar" noises.

Today the word "barbarian" often describes someone considered uncivilized, but its original meaning was much simpler. Ancient Greeks used the term to describe people who did not speak Greek because foreign languages sounded to them like repetitive "bar bar bar" noises. The word carried little connection to violence at first and mainly distinguished outsiders from Greek speakers. Over time, especially during conflicts with neighboring civilizations, the meaning gradually shifted toward describing people viewed as uncultured or savage. The Romans later adopted the term and expanded its negative connotations throughout Europe. This evolution shows how language can transform neutral descriptions into powerful labels influenced by culture, politics, and history.

Did you know?

The Greek word barbaros originally referred to non-Greek speakers and probably imitated speech that sounded like repeated “bar-bar” to Greek ears.
