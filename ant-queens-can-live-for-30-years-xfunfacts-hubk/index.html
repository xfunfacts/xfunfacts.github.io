Ant Queens Can Live for 30 Years xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] While worker ants often live only a short time, a queen can survive for decades and rule the same colony for years.

Ant queens are among the longest-lived insects on Earth, with some species surviving for up to 30 years under favorable conditions. This remarkable lifespan stands in sharp contrast to worker ants, which often live only a few months or several years depending on the species. The queen's primary role is reproduction, spending much of her life laying eggs that maintain and expand the colony. Protected by thousands or even millions of worker ants, queens experience fewer environmental dangers than other colony members, contributing to their exceptional longevity. Scientists continue studying ant queens because they appear to resist many of the biological processes associated with aging despite their enormous reproductive output. Understanding the genetic and physiological mechanisms behind their long lives may eventually provide insights into aging, immunity, and longevity in other organisms. Their extraordinary lifespan highlights the remarkable complexity and specialization found within highly organized insect societies.

Did you know?

Some ant queens establish new colonies after a mating flight, shedding their wings and using stored energy reserves to raise their first generation of workers.
