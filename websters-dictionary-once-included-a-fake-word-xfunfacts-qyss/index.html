Webster's Dictionary Once Included a Fake Word xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Webster's Dictionary accidentally included a fake word for five years before editors realized it had never existed.

Even dictionaries, which are trusted as authoritative references on language, can occasionally contain mistakes. One of the most famous examples involved the mysterious word "Dord," which appeared in Webster's New International Dictionary for approximately five years despite never actually existing as a real English word. The error originated from a clerical misunderstanding during the editing process. Editors intended to indicate that the abbreviation "D" stood for density, but handwritten notes were mistakenly interpreted as a completely new word. As a result, "Dord" entered the dictionary with its own definition before anyone realized the mistake. Once discovered, the fictional entry was removed from later editions, becoming one of publishing's most famous editorial errors. The incident is frequently cited in discussions about lexicography, proofreading, and the challenges involved in producing large reference works containing hundreds of thousands of entries.

Did you know?

The ghost word “dord” entered Webster’s New International Dictionary in 1934 after “D or d,” meaning an abbreviation for density, was mistakenly interpreted as a word; it was removed in 1947. merriam-webster.com
