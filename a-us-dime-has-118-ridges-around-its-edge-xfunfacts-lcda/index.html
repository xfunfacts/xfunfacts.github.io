A U.S. Dime Has 118 Ridges Around Its Edge xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] A U.S. dime has 118 ridges around its edge, a design originally intended to discourage coin shaving.

The edge of a United States dime contains 118 ridges, known as reeds, which were originally introduced to discourage people from shaving tiny amounts of precious metal from valuable coins. Although modern dimes are no longer made from solid silver, the ridged edge remains as a traditional security feature that also makes coins easier to identify by touch. Similar reeded edges appear on many coins around the world for both practical and historical reasons.

Did you know?

Reeded coin edges originally helped reveal the removal of precious metal from gold and silver coins; today's U.S. dime retains 118 reeds even though its modern composition contains no silver.
