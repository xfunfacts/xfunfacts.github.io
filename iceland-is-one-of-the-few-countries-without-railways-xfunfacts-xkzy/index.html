Iceland Is One of the Few Countries Without Railways xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Iceland is famous for volcanoes, glaciers, and modern living—but not a single public railway crosses the country.

Iceland is one of the few developed countries in the world without a public railway network. Unlike many nations that rely on trains for transportation, Iceland depends almost entirely on well-maintained roads, private vehicles, buses, and domestic flights. Several factors explain this unusual situation. The country's relatively small population, rugged volcanic terrain, glaciers, and scattered communities make building and maintaining railways expensive compared to other forms of transportation. Instead, Iceland invested heavily in its road system, including the famous Ring Road that circles most of the island. Although railway proposals have occasionally been discussed, none have moved beyond the planning stage. Visitors often explore the country by rental car, allowing them to access waterfalls, volcanoes, glaciers, and geothermal attractions at their own pace. Iceland's transportation system demonstrates that modern infrastructure can succeed without railways when geography and population create different priorities.

Did you know?

Iceland relies mainly on roads, domestic aviation, and maritime transport because it has no operational public passenger railway, although small industrial railway systems existed historically.
