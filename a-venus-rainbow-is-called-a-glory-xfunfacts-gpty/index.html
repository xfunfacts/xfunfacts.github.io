A Venus Rainbow Is Called a Glory xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] A colorful atmospheric phenomenon called a glory forms through light interference, creating rings that differ from ordinary rainbows.

The colorful rings sometimes seen around an observer's shadow in clouds or mist are called a glory, an optical phenomenon that differs from an ordinary rainbow. Unlike rainbows, which form through refraction and reflection inside raindrops, a glory is produced by the interference and backscattering of light within tiny water droplets. Glories are most commonly observed from airplanes looking down on clouds or from mountaintops when sunlight shines behind the observer. The effect creates concentric rings of vivid color surrounding the shadow, making it one of the atmosphere's most beautiful and least familiar natural displays.

Did you know?

A glory forms when sunlight is backscattered by tiny droplets, producing colored rings around the observer's antisolar point rather than a conventional rainbow arc.
