Pluto Is Sometimes Closer to the Sun Than Neptune xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Pluto occasionally sneaks closer to the Sun than Neptune, despite spending most of its long journey much farther away.

Although Pluto is now classified as a dwarf planet, it continues to surprise astronomers with its unusual orbit. Unlike the nearly circular paths followed by most planets, Pluto travels around the Sun in a highly elliptical orbit that is also tilted relative to the rest of the solar system. Because of this unique path, Pluto actually spends part of its orbit closer to the Sun than Neptune. The most recent period lasted from 1979 until 1999, during which Pluto temporarily became the eighth most distant object rather than the ninth. Despite crossing Neptune's orbital distance, there is no danger of the two worlds colliding because their orbits are carefully synchronized through gravitational resonance. Pluto completes one orbit around the Sun approximately every 248 Earth years, meaning these close approaches occur only during specific portions of its long journey. Its unusual motion continues to provide valuable insights into the formation and evolution of the outer solar system.

Did you know?

From 1979 to 1999, Pluto was closer to the Sun than Neptune because Pluto's eccentric orbit temporarily carried it inside Neptune's average orbital distance.
