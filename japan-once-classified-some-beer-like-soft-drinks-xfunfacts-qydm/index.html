Japan Once Classified Some Beer Like Soft Drinks xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Until 2013, some low-alcohol beers in Japan were regulated similarly to soft drinks. The classification reflected unique legal and tax rules.

Until 2013, Japan used regulations that treated certain alcoholic beverages containing less than ten percent alcohol differently from stronger alcoholic drinks. In some legal contexts, these beverages were classified similarly to soft drinks, reflecting the country's unique taxation and regulatory framework at the time. The classification influenced how some products were marketed and sold, although they still contained alcohol and were intended for adult consumers. Japan also has a long tradition of enjoying beverages in public parks during seasonal celebrations such as cherry blossom viewing, making low-alcohol drinks a familiar part of social gatherings. Regulatory changes over time have updated how these products are categorized, but the earlier rules remain an interesting example of how legal definitions can vary significantly between countries.
