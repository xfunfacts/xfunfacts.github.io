The UK's Royal Motto Is Written in French xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The royal motto of the United Kingdom isn't written in English. It still appears in French on the Royal Coat of Arms.

The official motto displayed on the Royal Coat of Arms of the United Kingdom is written not in English but in French. The phrase "Dieu et mon droit" translates as "God and my right," expressing the traditional belief that the monarch's authority ultimately comes from God. The motto dates back to the reign of King Richard I during the twelfth century, when French was widely spoken by England's ruling Norman elite. Alongside another French phrase, "Honi soit qui mal y pense," the motto reflects the lasting influence of Norman French on British royal traditions, heraldry, and legal history. Although English eventually became the country's dominant language, these historic French expressions remained part of the monarchy's official symbols for centuries. Today, the Royal Coat of Arms appears on government buildings, official documents, passports, and courtrooms throughout the United Kingdom, preserving a fascinating linguistic connection to medieval European history.

Did you know?

The royal motto “Dieu et mon droit,” meaning “God and my right,” reflects the monarchy's medieval French heritage, while “Honi soit qui mal y pense” belongs to the Order of the Garter.
