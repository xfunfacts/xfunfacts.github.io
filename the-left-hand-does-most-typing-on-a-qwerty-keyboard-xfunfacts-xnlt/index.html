The Left Hand Does Most Typing on a QWERTY Keyboard xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The left hand performs slightly more keystrokes on a QWERTY keyboard because many common English letters are on the left side.

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, the left hand performs slightly more keystrokes than the right during typical English typing. This happens because many commonly used letters, including E, R, T, A, S, D, and F, are located on the left side of the keyboard. The QWERTY layout was originally designed in the nineteenth century to reduce mechanical typewriter jams by separating frequently used letter combinations. Although alternative layouts such as Dvorak and Colemak aim to improve efficiency, QWERTY remains the world's most widely used keyboard arrangement.

Did you know?

Typing-load studies on QWERTY often find that the left hand performs slightly more keystrokes in English, though the exact balance depends on the text, technique, and keyboard layout.
