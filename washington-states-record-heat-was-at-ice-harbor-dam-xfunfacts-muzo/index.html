Washington State's Record Heat Was at Ice Harbor Dam xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Washington is known for cool weather, but its hottest recorded temperature reached a scorching 118°F at Ice Harbor Dam.

Washington State is often associated with cool weather, forests, and frequent rainfall, yet it has also experienced astonishingly high temperatures. The hottest temperature ever officially recorded in the state reached 118 degrees Fahrenheit, or 47.8 degrees Celsius, at Ice Harbor Dam on August 5, 1961. Located in the state's dry southeastern region, the area has a climate that differs dramatically from the cooler coastal environments around Seattle and the Olympic Peninsula. Eastern Washington receives much less rainfall and can experience intense summer heat due to its inland location and desert-like conditions. The record highlights how a single state can contain several distinct climate zones influenced by mountains, elevation, and distance from the Pacific Ocean. Although Washington's reputation remains closely tied to mild weather, historical temperature records demonstrate that extreme heat is entirely possible under the right atmospheric conditions. The event serves as an important reminder that regional climate varies considerably even within the same state.

Did you know?

Washington's official heat record has been tied at multiple locations, so individual claims about the state's single hottest site should be checked against current state records.
