It Literally Snows Metal on Venus xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Venus is so extreme that scientists believe metal can freeze and fall from its mountains like snow.

Venus is one of the most hostile planets in the solar system, yet its extreme environment produces an astonishing phenomenon often described as metallic snow. The planet's surface reaches temperatures hot enough to melt lead, making ordinary snowfall impossible. However, scientists believe that at the cooler elevations of Venus's highest mountains, certain metallic compounds vaporize in the intense heat before later condensing into frost-like deposits. Two minerals thought to participate in this process are galena, a lead sulfide mineral, and bismuthinite, which contains bismuth. Instead of frozen water crystals, these metallic substances may settle onto mountaintops much like snow accumulates on Earth's mountains. Evidence for this process comes from radar observations showing unusually reflective peaks, suggesting the presence of metallic coatings. Although no spacecraft has directly observed metal snow falling, planetary scientists consider the explanation one of the most likely reasons for these unusual radar signatures. This extraordinary phenomenon highlights how different planetary environments can produce weather unlike anything experienced on Earth.

Did you know?

Radar observations suggest some highlands on Venus contain reflective minerals, but the idea of metallic "snow" remains a hypothesis rather than direct observation.
