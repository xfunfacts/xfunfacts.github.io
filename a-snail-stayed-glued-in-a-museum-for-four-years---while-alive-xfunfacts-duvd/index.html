A Snail Stayed Glued in a Museum for Four Years - While Alive xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] A museum snail was accidentally glued to a display card and remained there for four years before anyone realized it was still alive.

One of the most remarkable museum stories involves a tiny snail that remained alive despite being mistakenly treated as a preserved specimen. During the nineteenth century, a snail at the British Museum was glued onto a specimen card as though it were dead. For approximately four years, no one realized it was still alive until scientists later observed signs of movement. Land snails can enter long periods of dormancy known as estivation, dramatically slowing their metabolism to survive unfavorable conditions. This extraordinary biological adaptation likely allowed the snail to endure the unusual circumstances. The story has become one of the most famous examples of the resilience of these small animals and demonstrates how remarkable survival strategies can sometimes surprise even experienced scientists.

Did you know?

Some snails can enter extremely long periods of dormancy during harsh conditions, slowing their metabolism so dramatically that survival can last for years.
