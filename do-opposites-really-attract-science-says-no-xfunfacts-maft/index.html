Do Opposites Really Attract? Science Says No xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Opposites may make entertaining movies, but real relationships are far more likely to grow between people who are alike.

The saying "opposites attract" has become deeply rooted in popular culture, but decades of psychological research suggest that similarity plays a much larger role in successful relationships. Studies consistently show that people tend to feel more attracted to individuals who share similar values, interests, educational backgrounds, personalities, and life goals. Familiarity often creates a sense of comfort, trust, and understanding that supports long-term compatibility. Some research even indicates that people are naturally drawn toward partners who resemble family members in subtle physical or behavioral ways, although this occurs unconsciously rather than intentionally. While differences can certainly make relationships exciting and help partners learn from one another, extreme opposites often create greater challenges in communication and decision-making. Psychologists emphasize that healthy relationships typically balance shared values with complementary strengths rather than complete opposites. The enduring popularity of the phrase reflects its appeal as a romantic idea, but scientific evidence suggests that common ground usually provides a stronger foundation for lasting relationships.

Did you know?

Large studies generally find that romantic partners resemble each other on many attitudes, habits, and social characteristics, offering more support for similarity than for "opposites attract."
