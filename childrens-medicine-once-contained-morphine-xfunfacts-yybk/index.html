Children's Medicine Once Contained Morphine xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Some children's medicines once contained morphine before modern drug safety standards and regulations were introduced.

During the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, some medicines sold for children contained morphine or other opioid ingredients to reduce pain, suppress coughing, or help children sleep. At the time, the dangers of addiction and overdose were not fully understood, and medicines faced far less government regulation than they do today. Modern medical research eventually revealed these serious risks, leading to much stricter testing, labeling, and safety standards. The history serves as an important reminder of how medical knowledge continues to evolve.

Did you know?

Patent medicines for children once contained opiates such as morphine or laudanum, helping drive later reforms in drug labeling, safety testing, and pharmaceutical regulation.
