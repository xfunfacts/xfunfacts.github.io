Polar Bears Hunt Walruses by Causing Stampedes xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Polar bears sometimes hunt enormous walruses by creating chaos, triggering deadly stampedes instead of attacking directly.

Polar bears are powerful predators, but they do not always rely on direct attacks when hunting large prey such as walruses. Adult walruses are heavily built, protected by thick skin and long tusks, making them dangerous opponents even for the Arctic's largest land predator. Instead of confronting a healthy adult individually, polar bears sometimes charge toward crowded groups resting on beaches or ice, causing panic and triggering a stampede. During the chaos, young, weak, or injured walruses may be crushed beneath the weight of the fleeing herd. After the stampede subsides, the bear feeds on the animals that did not survive. This strategy allows the predator to obtain food while avoiding the considerable risk of fighting an adult walrus directly. Such behavior demonstrates the remarkable adaptability and intelligence of polar bears, which modify their hunting techniques depending on prey, environmental conditions, and opportunities available in the harsh Arctic ecosystem.

Did you know?

Walruses can weigh well over a ton and have formidable tusks, so polar bears usually target vulnerable individuals rather than healthy adults.
