Why Oxymoron Is an Oxymoron xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The word "oxymoron" is itself an oxymoron because its Greek roots combine the meanings "sharp" and "dull." Its name perfectly illustrates its own definition.

The word "oxymoron" provides a fascinating example of language describing itself. Derived from Ancient Greek, it combines the words "oxy," meaning sharp or keen, and "moros," meaning dull or foolish. Because the two roots express opposite ideas, the word itself functions as an oxymoron, a figure of speech that joins contradictory terms together. English contains many familiar examples, including phrases like "deafening silence," "bittersweet," and "organized chaos." Linguists appreciate oxymorons because they demonstrate how seemingly conflicting ideas can create richer meaning when combined. The term's own contradictory origin makes it one of the most amusing and self-referential words in the English language.

Did you know?

“Oxymoron” comes from Greek roots meaning roughly “sharp” and “dull” or “foolish,” making the word itself an etymological combination of contrasting ideas.
