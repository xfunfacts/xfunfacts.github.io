Russia Missed the Olympics Because of a Calendar xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Russia arrived nearly two weeks late to the 1908 Olympics because it was still following a different calendar than the rest of Europe.

The 1908 Olympic Games in London produced one of the most unusual scheduling mishaps in sports history when the Russian team reportedly arrived twelve days late because of a calendar difference. At the time, Russia still officially followed the Julian calendar, while most of Europe had already adopted the Gregorian calendar used today. By the early twentieth century, the two calendar systems differed by twelve days due to variations in calculating leap years over centuries. As a result, athletes and officials relying on the Julian calendar mistakenly interpreted the Olympic schedule and reached London after many events had already begun. Russia did not officially switch to the Gregorian calendar until 1918 following the Russian Revolution, bringing its dates into alignment with most of the rest of Europe. The incident remains one of history's most memorable examples of how something as simple as differing calendar systems can create significant international confusion. It also illustrates how global standardization became increasingly important as travel, communication, and international competition expanded.

Did you know?

The story that Russia missed the start of the 1908 Olympics because of the Julian calendar is widely repeated, but historians dispute whether it actually happened.
