Dry Ice Turns Directly into Gas xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Dry ice changes directly from a solid into carbon dioxide gas through sublimation, leaving no liquid behind.

Dry ice changes directly from a solid into carbon dioxide gas through a process known as sublimation, completely skipping the liquid phase under normal atmospheric pressure. Because of this unique property, dry ice produces the familiar white fog often seen in theatrical performances, scientific demonstrations, and food transportation. The visible cloud is actually condensed water vapor formed as the extremely cold carbon dioxide cools the surrounding air. Dry ice has a surface temperature of about minus 78.5 degrees Celsius, making it cold enough to cause frostbite if handled without protective gloves. It is widely used for refrigeration during shipping because it keeps products cold without leaving behind liquid water as it disappears.

Did you know?

At normal atmospheric pressure, dry ice sublimates at about -78.5°C, which is why it produces cold carbon dioxide gas without forming a puddle.
