Australia Produced the World's First Feature Film xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The world's first feature-length movie wasn't made in Hollywood. It premiered in Australia more than a century ago.

The world's first documented feature-length motion picture was produced in Australia in 1906, marking a groundbreaking moment in cinema history. Titled The Story of the Kelly Gang, the film told the story of the infamous Australian outlaw Ned Kelly and ran for well over an hour, making it significantly longer than the short films common during the early years of filmmaking. Its ambitious storytelling demonstrated that audiences were willing to watch extended narratives rather than brief moving images. Although much of the original film has been lost, surviving fragments and historical records confirm its enormous importance in the development of modern cinema. The production helped establish Australia as an early pioneer in filmmaking and influenced the future evolution of feature-length storytelling around the world. Today, The Story of the Kelly Gang remains one of the most significant milestones in the history of motion pictures.

Did you know?

Australia's The Story of the Kelly Gang premiered in 1906 and is widely recognized as the world's first feature-length narrative film.
