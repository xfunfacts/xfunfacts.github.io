Owls Can't Move Their Eyes in Their Sockets xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Owls cannot move their eyes inside their sockets, so they rotate their heads to scan their surroundings with remarkable precision.

Unlike humans, owls cannot move their eyes within their sockets because their large tube-shaped eyes are held almost completely in place by bone. Instead of shifting their eyes, owls rotate their heads to change what they see, reaching angles of up to about 270 degrees. Special adaptations in their blood vessels and neck structure allow this remarkable movement without interrupting blood flow to the brain. Combined with excellent night vision and highly sensitive hearing, this gives owls extraordinary hunting abilities in darkness.

Did you know?

Owl eyes are elongated and held in place by bony structures called sclerotic rings, which is why the eyes cannot rotate freely in their sockets.
