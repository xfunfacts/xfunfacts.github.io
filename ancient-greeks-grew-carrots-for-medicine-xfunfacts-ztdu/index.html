Ancient Greeks Grew Carrots for Medicine xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Ancient Greeks first cultivated carrots for medicinal purposes long before they became one of the world's favorite vegetables.

The ancient Greeks originally cultivated carrots for their medicinal properties rather than as an everyday food. Early carrots looked very different from the familiar orange varieties seen today and were often purple, yellow, white, or even black. Ancient herbalists believed carrots could help treat digestive problems and other ailments. Over centuries of selective breeding, carrots became sweeter, larger, and more suitable as a vegetable, eventually becoming one of the world's most widely grown root crops.

Did you know?

Ancient Greek medical writers described wild carrot and its seeds as herbal remedies, but the claim that Greeks first domesticated carrots specifically for medicine is not firmly established.
