Most of Taste Actually Comes From Smell xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Up to 80% of what we experience as flavor actually comes from smell. This explains why food tastes bland when your nose is blocked.

Most people think taste comes entirely from the tongue, but smell actually provides the majority of the flavor we experience while eating. As food is chewed, aromatic compounds travel through the back of the throat to receptors inside the nose, where the brain combines smell with signals from the tongue to create complex flavors. This is why many foods seem bland when you have a cold or when you pinch your nose shut. The tongue itself detects only a limited number of basic tastes, including sweet, salty, sour, bitter, and umami. Everything else depends heavily on the sense of smell. Scientists continue studying this close partnership between taste and smell because it plays an important role in nutrition, food enjoyment, and even memory. The connection explains why familiar aromas can instantly remind people of childhood meals or meaningful life experiences.

Did you know?

Smell contributes greatly to flavor through retronasal olfaction, but the popular claim that exactly 80% of taste comes from smell is an oversimplification.
