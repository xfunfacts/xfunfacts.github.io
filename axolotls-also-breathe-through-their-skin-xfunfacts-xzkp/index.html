Axolotls Also Breathe Through Their Skin xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Axolotls breathe through both their gills and skin, with simple lungs providing additional oxygen when needed.

In addition to breathing through their external gills, axolotls also absorb oxygen directly through their skin. This process, known as cutaneous respiration, supplements oxygen intake and helps them survive in different aquatic conditions. They can also gulp air at the water's surface when necessary, using simple lungs inherited from their amphibian ancestors. Employing multiple breathing methods provides valuable flexibility in changing environments. This combination of respiratory adaptations contributes to the axolotl's resilience.

Did you know?

Axolotls can survive with surprisingly low oxygen levels, an ability that helps them live in cool, slow-moving freshwater habitats around Mexico City.
