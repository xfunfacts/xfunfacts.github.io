The U.S. Dollar Became America's Official Currency in 1785 xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The United States adopted the dollar as its official currency in 1785, making it one of the world's oldest national currencies.

The United States officially adopted the dollar as its national monetary unit in 1785, making it one of the earliest countries to establish a decimal-based currency system. Inspired partly by the widely circulated Spanish dollar, the new currency helped create a standardized financial system for the young nation. Over time, the U.S. dollar became one of the world's most influential currencies, widely used in international trade, banking, and global finance. Modern U.S. currency includes both coins and paper notes featuring important historical figures and national symbols. The dollar continues to play a central role in the global economy, serving as a major reserve currency for many countries.

Did you know?

The Continental Congress adopted the dollar as the United States' monetary unit in July 1785, while the Coinage Act of 1792 subsequently established the U.S. Mint and a decimal-based national coinage system.
