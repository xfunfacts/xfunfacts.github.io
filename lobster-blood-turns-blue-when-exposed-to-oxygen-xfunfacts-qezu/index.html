Lobster Blood Turns Blue When Exposed to Oxygen xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Lobster blood turns blue when exposed to oxygen because it contains copper-based hemocyanin instead of hemoglobin.

Lobster blood appears nearly colorless inside the body, but it turns blue when exposed to oxygen because it contains hemocyanin instead of the iron-based hemoglobin found in human blood. Hemocyanin uses copper atoms to transport oxygen, producing a distinctive blue color after oxygen binds to the molecule. This adaptation is common among many crustaceans and several other invertebrates. Different oxygen-carrying molecules evolved independently in different animal groups, illustrating the remarkable diversity of life on Earth.

Did you know?

Lobster hemolymph uses copper-containing hemocyanin to transport oxygen, giving oxygenated hemolymph a bluish color unlike the red hemoglobin-based blood of humans.
