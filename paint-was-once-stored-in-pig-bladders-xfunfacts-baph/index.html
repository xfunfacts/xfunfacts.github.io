Paint Was Once Stored in Pig Bladders xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Artists once stored paint inside cleaned pig bladders before portable metal paint tubes transformed painting.

Before portable metal paint tubes were invented, artists often stored paint inside cleaned pig bladders. The flexible bladders could hold oil paint until the artist punctured them to squeeze out the desired amount. Although effective, they were messy and prone to leaking. The invention of collapsible metal paint tubes in the nineteenth century transformed painting by making colors easier to transport outdoors. This innovation played an important role in the rise of Impressionist artists, who could paint directly in natural landscapes.

Did you know?

Before collapsible metal paint tubes appeared in the 1840s, artists commonly stored prepared paint in animal bladders that had to be punctured to access the color.
