Ocean Algae Produce Nearly Half of Earth's Oxygen xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Ocean algae produce nearly half of Earth's oxygen through photosynthesis, supporting life across the planet.

Marine algae and microscopic ocean organisms produce nearly half of the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere through photosynthesis, making them among the planet's most important life-support systems. Tiny phytoplankton floating near the ocean's surface absorb sunlight, carbon dioxide, and nutrients to produce oxygen while forming the foundation of marine food webs. These microscopic organisms support countless species, from tiny zooplankton to enormous whales. Because ocean ecosystems play such a vital role in regulating Earth's climate and atmosphere, scientists closely monitor changes caused by warming oceans, pollution, and acidification. Protecting marine environments helps preserve not only ocean biodiversity but also one of the world's greatest natural sources of oxygen.

Did you know?

Marine photosynthetic organisms, especially phytoplankton, generate roughly half of global oxygen production, although much of that oxygen is also consumed within the ocean.
