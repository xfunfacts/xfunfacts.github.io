Astronauts Can't Burp Normally in Space xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Burping works differently in space because gas and liquid no longer separate inside the stomach. Astronauts call the result a "wet burp."

Astronauts cannot burp the same way they do on Earth because microgravity changes how gases and liquids behave inside the stomach. Under normal gravity, gas naturally rises above food and liquid, making it easy to release with a burp. In space, everything floats together instead of separating into layers. As a result, astronauts often experience what is known as a "wet burp," where gas and small amounts of liquid come up at the same time. This unusual effect is one of many digestive changes caused by weightlessness and is carefully considered when planning astronauts' diets during space missions.

Did you know?

In microgravity, gas and liquid do not separate normally in the stomach, so a burp can bring liquid upward with the gas, creating what astronauts informally call a “wet burp.”
