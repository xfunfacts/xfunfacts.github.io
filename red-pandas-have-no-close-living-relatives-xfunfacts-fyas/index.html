Red Pandas Have No Close Living Relatives xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Despite the name, the red panda isn't closely related to the giant panda and belongs to an animal family all its own.

Despite sharing part of its name with the giant panda, the red panda belongs to an entirely different branch of the animal family tree. Scientists classify the red panda within its own unique family, Ailuridae, making it the only living representative of that group. Although red pandas and giant pandas both eat bamboo, genetic studies show that this similarity evolved independently rather than through close shared ancestry. Red pandas are actually more closely related to groups including raccoons, weasels, and skunks than to bears. Their bushy tails, climbing abilities, and specialized wrists help them thrive in the forests of the Himalayas and southwestern China. Their evolutionary uniqueness makes conservation especially important because losing the species would mean the extinction of an entire animal family.

Did you know?

Red pandas belong to the family Ailuridae, and they are its only living members, making them unusually isolated on the mammalian family tree.
