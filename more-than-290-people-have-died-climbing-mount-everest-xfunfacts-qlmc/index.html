More Than 290 People Have Died Climbing Mount Everest xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Climbing Mount Everest remains one of the world's greatest adventures, but more than 290 people have lost their lives attempting the summit.

Mount Everest represents the ultimate challenge for mountaineers, but it is also one of the world's most dangerous climbing destinations. Since organized expeditions began in the early twentieth century, more than 290 climbers have lost their lives attempting to reach or descend from the summit. The mountain presents numerous hazards, including avalanches, falling ice, extreme weather, altitude sickness, exhaustion, and lack of oxygen. Above approximately 8,000 meters lies the infamous "death zone," where the air contains so little oxygen that the human body gradually begins to deteriorate even while resting. Recovering bodies from these elevations is often too dangerous, expensive, or physically impossible, leaving many climbers permanently on the mountain. Some have even become well-known landmarks that later expeditions recognize while ascending. Despite the risks, hundreds of climbers continue attempting Everest each year, driven by the challenge of standing atop the world's highest peak. The mountain serves as both a symbol of human determination and a reminder that nature's most spectacular places often demand extraordinary respect and caution.

Did you know?

More than 300 climbers have died on Mount Everest since expeditions began, with major dangers including avalanches, falls, extreme cold, altitude sickness, and oxygen deprivation in the death zone.
