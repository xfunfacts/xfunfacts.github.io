A Shrimp's Organs Float in Its Blood xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Shrimp have an open circulatory system where many internal organs are surrounded directly by circulating hemolymph.

Unlike humans, shrimp have an open circulatory system instead of a network of enclosed blood vessels. Their heart pumps blood-like fluid called hemolymph into body cavities, where it flows around internal organs before returning to the heart. This means many of a shrimp's organs are surrounded directly by circulating fluid rather than being enclosed within arteries and veins. Open circulatory systems are common among many invertebrates, including insects and crustaceans.

Did you know?

Shrimp use hemocyanin to transport oxygen in their hemolymph, a copper-based molecule that can appear bluish when oxygenated.
