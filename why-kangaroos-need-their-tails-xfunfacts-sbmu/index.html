Why Kangaroos Need Their Tails xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Kangaroos rely on their powerful tails for balance and movement while hopping. Without tail support, they cannot hop normally.

A kangaroo's powerful tail is far more than a balancing aid. It functions as an essential part of the animal's locomotion, providing propulsion and stability during hopping. Researchers have shown that the tail acts almost like an additional leg, pushing against the ground to help generate forward movement. If a kangaroo's tail is lifted off the ground, it cannot hop normally because the coordinated movement between its hind legs and tail is disrupted. Kangaroos that lose their tails through injury can still move, but they do so much more slowly and often require significant adaptation. The tail's unique role makes kangaroos one of the few animals that actively use their tails as a major source of locomotor power.

Did you know?

Kangaroo tails act almost like a fifth limb during slow movement, supporting body weight and helping propel the animal forward while it grazes.
