There Are Over 130 Species of Owls xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Over 130 owl species inhabit forests, deserts, and grasslands, each with specialized hunting skills and remarkable night vision.

More than 130 species of owls are known worldwide, occupying habitats that range from frozen Arctic tundra to tropical forests and open grasslands. These birds are highly specialized nocturnal predators with extraordinary hearing and excellent low-light vision. Unlike most birds, an owl's eyes are fixed in their sockets, so it compensates by rotating its head up to 270 degrees to scan its surroundings. Soft-edged feathers allow owls to fly almost silently, giving prey very little warning before an attack. Different owl species hunt rodents, insects, fish, reptiles, and even other birds depending on their size and habitat. By controlling rodent populations, owls provide important ecological benefits for both natural ecosystems and agriculture. Their mysterious appearance and haunting calls have made them symbols of wisdom, mystery, and folklore in cultures around the world for thousands of years.

Did you know?

Owls vary greatly in size, from tiny elf owls to large eagle-owls, yet nearly all are highly specialized predators with exceptional hearing.
