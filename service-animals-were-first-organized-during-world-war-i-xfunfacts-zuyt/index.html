Service Animals Were First Organized During World War I xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Service animals became an organized profession while helping wounded soldiers return to everyday life during World War I.

Organized service animal programs began taking shape during World War I, when Germany developed formal training programs to help soldiers who had lost their vision in combat. These specially trained dogs assisted veterans with daily tasks, navigation, and independence, laying the foundation for the modern guide dog programs used around the world today. However, the relationship between humans and assistance animals extends much further into history. Historical records dating back to the mid-16th century describe animals helping people with disabilities in various ways, although these efforts were informal and not part of structured training organizations. Since World War I, service animals have expanded far beyond guide dogs and now assist people with mobility challenges, hearing impairments, medical conditions, and psychological disabilities. Their remarkable abilities continue to improve quality of life for millions of individuals worldwide.

Did you know?

Organized guide-dog training expanded after World War I to assist blinded veterans, although humans had used working and assistance animals long before the war.
