NASA's Curiosity Rover Takes Selfies on Mars xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] NASA's Curiosity rover creates detailed selfies by combining many images captured with its robotic arm-mounted camera.

NASA's Curiosity rover creates its famous selfies by combining dozens of separate photographs taken with a camera mounted on the end of its robotic arm. The arm itself is positioned differently for each image, allowing software to remove it from the final composite and produce the illusion that the rover photographed itself. These images serve important scientific purposes by documenting the rover's condition, monitoring dust accumulation, and recording the surrounding landscape after completing exploration activities. Curiosity has been exploring Gale Crater since 2012, continuing to reveal evidence that ancient Mars once offered conditions capable of supporting microbial life.

Did you know?

Curiosity's selfies are mosaics assembled from many close-up images, and the robotic arm is positioned so carefully that it often disappears from the final composite.
