Movie Snacks Around the World Are Surprisingly Different xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Movie snacks vary widely around the world. Some countries prefer roasted insects, dried cuttlefish, or salted plums instead of popcorn.

For many Americans, popcorn is almost inseparable from going to the movies, but snack traditions vary widely around the world. In Colombia, some moviegoers enjoy roasted ants as a crunchy treat, while cinemas in South Korea often sell dried cuttlefish, a chewy seafood snack with a smoky flavor. In China, dried salted plums are a popular alternative, offering a sweet and tangy taste unlike typical Western movie snacks. Other countries also have their own favorites, reflecting local tastes and culinary traditions rather than following a single global standard. These differences remind us that foods often considered normal in one culture may seem surprising in another. Visiting movie theaters abroad can therefore become an unexpected opportunity to experience regional cuisine while enjoying the same films watched by audiences around the world.

Did you know?

Japanese cinemas commonly sell popcorn and hot dogs, while some theaters across Asia offer local snacks such as dried seafood or flavored crackers, reflecting regional food preferences rather than a universal movie menu.
