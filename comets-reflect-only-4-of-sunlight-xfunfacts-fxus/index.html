Comets Reflect Only 4% of Sunlight xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Comets may shine brightly in the sky, but their surfaces reflect only about 4% of sunlight, making them darker than most people imagine.

Although comets often appear bright in the night sky, they are surprisingly dark objects. Their solid surfaces reflect only about four percent of the sunlight that reaches them, making them darker than fresh asphalt in many cases. Most of the brightness people observe actually comes from the glowing cloud of gas and dust, known as the coma, that forms as a comet approaches the Sun. Heat causes frozen materials inside the comet to vaporize, releasing particles that reflect sunlight far more effectively than the comet's surface itself. Scientists believe the dark exterior results from carbon-rich compounds and dust accumulated over billions of years. This contrast between an extremely dark nucleus and a brilliantly glowing tail explains why comets can become spectacular celestial objects despite reflecting very little direct sunlight.

Did you know?

Comet nuclei are among the darkest objects in the Solar System because their surfaces are coated with carbon-rich material and space-weathered dust.
