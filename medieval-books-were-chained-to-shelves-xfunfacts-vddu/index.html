Medieval Books Were Chained to Shelves xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Medieval libraries chained valuable books to shelves to prevent theft before the age of printing presses.

During the Middle Ages, books were among the most valuable possessions a library could own because each copy had to be written or copied by hand before the printing press existed. To prevent theft, many libraries attached books to shelves with metal chains while still allowing readers to open and study them. This practice reflected the enormous effort required to produce a single manuscript. Some chained libraries have survived and can still be visited today.

Did you know?

Chained libraries attached books to reading desks with metal chains, allowing visitors to consult valuable manuscripts while preventing their removal; surviving examples include Hereford Cathedral's library.
