Why Liechtenstein Changed Its Flag xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Liechtenstein added a crown to its national flag after discovering it matched Haiti's at the 1936 Olympics. The change made the flag unique.

At the 1936 Olympic Games, Liechtenstein discovered an unexpected problem: its national flag was identical to Haiti's. Both countries displayed simple horizontal blue and red designs, creating confusion during the international event. To ensure its flag would be instantly recognizable, Liechtenstein added a gold princely crown to the upper corner of the flag the following year. The crown symbolized the country's monarchy while permanently distinguishing its national flag from Haiti's design. This unusual episode remains one of the most famous examples of how international events have influenced the evolution of national symbols. Today, the crowned flag continues to represent Liechtenstein and serves as a reminder of an embarrassing but historically significant Olympic coincidence.

Did you know?

Liechtenstein added a golden crown to its blue-and-red national flag in 1937 after its similarity to Haiti's flag became apparent at the 1936 Olympics, giving the principality a distinctive national emblem.
