Hibernating Bears Do Not Urinate xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Hibernating bears recycle waste into protein, allowing them to avoid urinating throughout the winter.

Hibernating bears do not urinate during their long winter sleep because their bodies recycle waste products into useful proteins instead of eliminating them. This remarkable adaptation prevents muscle loss and conserves water while the bears remain inactive for months without eating or drinking. Their heart rate and metabolism also slow dramatically, reducing energy demands throughout hibernation. Scientists study these biological processes in hopes of improving treatments for muscle loss, kidney disease, and long-term inactivity in humans.

Did you know?

Hibernating bears can recycle nitrogen from urea into amino acids, helping them preserve muscle and body tissues during months without normal feeding.
