Writing Down Worries Before an Exam Can Improve Scores xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Feeling anxious before an exam? Research suggests writing your worries down first may help you stay calmer and perform better.

Feeling anxious before an important exam is common, but research suggests that a simple writing exercise may help improve performance. Studies have found that students who spend a few minutes writing down their worries before a test often experience lower anxiety and better concentration afterward. Putting stressful thoughts onto paper appears to reduce the mental effort required to suppress those concerns, leaving more attention available for solving problems during the exam. This technique is particularly helpful for individuals who experience test anxiety, as it allows the brain to organize emotions before focusing on academic tasks. While it does not replace proper preparation or studying, expressive writing is an inexpensive strategy supported by psychological research. The finding highlights how managing emotions can sometimes improve learning outcomes just as much as mastering the material itself.

Did you know?

In some experiments, writing about test worries for several minutes improved performance among highly anxious students, possibly by reducing the mental load created by intrusive thoughts.
