Most Vitamin C in Fruit Is Found Near the Skin xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Much of a fruit's vitamin C is found near the skin. Peeling too deeply can reduce valuable nutrients and antioxidants.

In many fruits, a large portion of the vitamin C is found in or just beneath the skin, making careful peeling an important factor in preserving nutrients. Fruits such as apples, citrus fruits, kiwis, and pears often contain valuable vitamins, fiber, and antioxidants close to their outer layers. Washing fruit thoroughly instead of removing thick layers of peel can help retain more of these beneficial compounds when the skin is edible. Vitamin C plays an essential role in supporting the immune system, helping produce collagen, promoting wound healing, and protecting cells from oxidative stress. Choosing fresh fruit and minimizing unnecessary peeling are simple ways to maximize nutritional value.

Did you know?

Vitamin C distribution varies by fruit species, with substantial concentrations sometimes found in the peel or outer tissues; however, peeling does not universally remove most of a fruit's vitamin C.
