Mercury and Venus Are the Only Planets Without Moons xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Every planet has at least one moon except two. Mercury and Venus stand alone for reasons that continue to interest astronomers.

Mercury and Venus are the only planets in our Solar System without natural moons. Every other planet, from Earth to Neptune, has at least one satellite, bringing the total number of confirmed planetary moons to well over 170. Some of these moons are surprisingly large. Jupiter's moon Ganymede and Saturn's moon Titan are both bigger than the planet Mercury, despite orbiting much larger worlds. Scientists believe Mercury and Venus likely lack moons because of their location near the Sun and the powerful gravitational forces that make stable satellite orbits difficult. Studying these differences helps astronomers better understand how planets and their moon systems formed billions of years ago. The absence of moons around Mercury and Venus makes them unique among the eight planets and provides important clues about the history of our Solar System.

Did you know?

Mercury and Venus may lack moons because of their proximity to the Sun, where strong solar gravity can make stable satellite orbits more difficult.
