Landlocked Mongolia Has a Navy xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Mongolia has no coastline, yet it still has a navy complete with a vessel and a tiny crew operating on a freshwater lake.

Mongolia is completely landlocked, yet it officially operates a navy. The country's tiny naval force consists of just one vessel, the Sukhbaatar III, and only a handful of personnel assigned to operate it. Rather than patrolling an ocean, the boat serves on Lake Khövsgöl, one of Mongolia's largest freshwater lakes located near the Russian border. Historically, the vessel has primarily been used for transporting cargo, supporting local operations, and assisting with activities on the lake rather than performing traditional military missions. Because Mongolia has no coastline, its navy is often cited as one of the world's smallest and most unusual armed forces. Despite its modest size, the existence of a naval unit reflects the practical need for water transportation and administration on major inland lakes. The story has become a favorite example of how national military organizations sometimes evolve to meet highly specific geographic circumstances.

Did you know?

Mongolia's Lake Khövsgöl has hosted a small vessel historically associated with the country's so-called navy, but Mongolia does not operate a conventional modern naval force, and its maritime activities are primarily civilian.
