Five Million Tons of Water Evaporate from the Dead Sea Each Day xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Around five million tons of water evaporate from the Dead Sea every day because of its hot, arid climate.

Around five million tons of water evaporate from the Dead Sea every day because of the region's intense heat, dry air, and low rainfall. Located at one of the lowest points on Earth's surface, the Dead Sea experiences extremely high evaporation rates that leave dissolved salts behind, making its water nearly ten times saltier than most oceans. This extraordinary salinity allows people to float easily while preventing most fish and aquatic plants from surviving there. As water continues to evaporate faster than it is replaced, the Dead Sea has gradually shrunk over recent decades, creating environmental challenges such as sinkholes and changing shorelines. Scientists continue studying the region to better understand water management, climate, and the effects of long-term evaporation in arid environments.

Did you know?

The Dead Sea has no natural outlet, so water primarily leaves through evaporation; reduced inflow from the Jordan River has contributed to its rapidly declining water level.
