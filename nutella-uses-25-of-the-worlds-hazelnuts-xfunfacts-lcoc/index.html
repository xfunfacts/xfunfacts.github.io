Nutella Uses 25% of the World’s Hazelnuts xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Nutella uses roughly one-quarter of the world's hazelnut supply. Its enormous popularity continues driving research into increasing global hazelnut production.

Nutella has become one of the world's most recognizable chocolate spreads, and its popularity has a remarkable impact on global agriculture. It is estimated that around one-quarter of the world's hazelnut harvest is used to produce Nutella, making the brand one of the largest consumers of hazelnuts anywhere. Most of these hazelnuts come from Turkey, which produces the majority of the global supply, although other countries also contribute. Because worldwide demand continues to grow, scientists and agricultural researchers are exploring ways to improve hazelnut farming through disease-resistant trees, better cultivation methods, and higher-yield varieties. Climate change has also increased interest in expanding hazelnut production to new regions. The story highlights how a single food product can influence international farming, research, and supply chains. Every jar of Nutella represents not only a popular breakfast spread but also a massive agricultural network connecting growers, manufacturers, and consumers across the globe.

Did you know?

Ferrero is one of the world's largest buyers of hazelnuts, sourcing them from countries including Turkey and Italy, but the claim that Nutella alone uses exactly 25% of global production is not consistently documented.
