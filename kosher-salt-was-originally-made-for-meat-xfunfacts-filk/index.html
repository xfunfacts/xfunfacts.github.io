Kosher Salt Was Originally Made for Meat xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Kosher salt earned its name from preparing meat under Jewish dietary laws long before it became a favorite ingredient in home kitchens.

Many people assume kosher salt earned its name simply because it follows Jewish dietary rules, but its original purpose was much more specific. Kosher salt was traditionally used during the koshering process, in which coarse salt draws blood from meat according to Jewish dietary law before cooking. Its large, flaky crystals make it particularly effective for absorbing surface moisture and blood while remaining easy to spread and remove. Over time, professional chefs embraced kosher salt because it contains no iodine or anti-caking agents, giving it a cleaner flavor and making it easier to control during seasoning. Today it has become one of the most widely used salts in professional kitchens around the world, even among cooks who do not follow kosher dietary practices. Its popularity reflects both its historical origins and its practical advantages in food preparation.

Did you know?

Kosher salt is named for its traditional use in koshering meat, where coarse salt draws out blood under Jewish dietary practices; the salt itself is not automatically certified kosher because of its name.
