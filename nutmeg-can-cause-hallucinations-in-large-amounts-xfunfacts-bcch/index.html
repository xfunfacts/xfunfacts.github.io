Nutmeg Can Cause Hallucinations in Large Amounts xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Large amounts of nutmeg can cause hallucinations because of myristicin, but they may also be dangerous.

Nutmeg contains a naturally occurring compound called myristicin that can produce hallucinations and other serious symptoms when consumed in very large amounts. However, reaching these effects typically requires far more nutmeg than is ever used in cooking and often causes severe nausea, dizziness, rapid heartbeat, and other unpleasant side effects before hallucinations occur. Medical professionals strongly advise against intentionally consuming excessive amounts because myristicin poisoning can be dangerous and may require emergency treatment. In normal culinary quantities, nutmeg remains a safe and widely enjoyed spice used in both sweet and savory dishes.

Did you know?

Large amounts of nutmeg can cause poisoning involving nausea, rapid heartbeat, agitation, confusion, and hallucinations; myristicin is one implicated compound, and symptoms may last more than a day.
