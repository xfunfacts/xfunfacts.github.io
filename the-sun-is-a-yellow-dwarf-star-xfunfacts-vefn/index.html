The Sun Is a Yellow Dwarf Star xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star, commonly called a yellow dwarf, and is expected to shine for billions more years.

Astronomers classify the Sun as a G-type main-sequence star, commonly referred to as a yellow dwarf. Despite the name, the Sun actually appears white when viewed from space, while Earth's atmosphere scatters blue light and gives it a yellow appearance from the ground. Main-sequence stars spend most of their lives converting hydrogen into helium through nuclear fusion, producing a steady supply of energy. The Sun has remained in this stable phase for about 4.6 billion years and is expected to continue for roughly another five billion years. Understanding the Sun's classification helps astronomers compare it with billions of other stars and better understand how stars evolve over time.

Did you know?

Despite the name "yellow dwarf," the Sun emits broadly across visible wavelengths and would appear essentially white when viewed from space.
