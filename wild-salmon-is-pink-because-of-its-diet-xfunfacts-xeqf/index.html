Wild Salmon Is Pink Because of Its Diet xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Wild salmon naturally become pink from eating shrimp and krill rich in carotenoid pigments. Farm-raised salmon receive similar pigments through their feed.

Wild salmon owe their distinctive pink flesh to their natural diet rather than their genetics alone. In the ocean, salmon regularly eat shrimp, krill, and other small crustaceans rich in carotenoid pigments, especially astaxanthin. These pigments accumulate in the fish's muscles over time, creating the familiar pink or reddish color. Farm-raised salmon, however, would naturally have much paler flesh because they do not consume the same diet in captivity. To match consumer expectations, fish farmers commonly add approved carotenoid pigments to salmon feed, producing a color similar to that of wild fish. The practice is carefully regulated in many countries and affects only appearance rather than basic nutritional value. This fascinating example demonstrates how an animal's diet can directly influence its physical appearance.

Did you know?

Salmon obtain the pigment astaxanthin from their diet, and this antioxidant accumulates in muscle tissue, giving the flesh its characteristic pink-orange color.
