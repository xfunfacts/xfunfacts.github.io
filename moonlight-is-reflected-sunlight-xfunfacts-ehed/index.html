Moonlight Is Reflected Sunlight xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Moonlight is simply sunlight reflected from the Moon's surface, creating the familiar glow seen in Earth's night sky.

Moonlight is not produced by the Moon itself. Instead, the Moon shines because its rocky surface reflects sunlight back toward Earth. Although it often appears bright in the night sky, the Moon reflects only about 12% of the sunlight that reaches it, making its surface much darker than many people expect. The changing angles between the Sun, Earth, and Moon create the familiar lunar phases, from the new moon to the full moon. Moonlight has guided travelers, influenced calendars, and inspired countless stories throughout human history. Scientists also study reflected sunlight from the Moon to learn more about its surface composition and geology. Understanding that moonlight is simply reflected sunlight helps explain why the Moon's brightness changes throughout the month and why eclipses occur during special alignments of Earth, the Moon, and the Sun.

Did you know?

The Moon reflects only a small fraction of the sunlight that reaches it, which is why its surface is actually much darker than it appears from Earth.
