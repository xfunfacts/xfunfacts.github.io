Black Women Secretly Served in the Civil War xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Many Black women served as nurses, scouts, and spies during the Civil War despite being officially barred from military service.

Although Black women were officially prohibited from serving as soldiers during the American Civil War, many found other vital ways to support the war effort. They worked as nurses caring for wounded soldiers, served as scouts gathering intelligence, carried secret messages, and sometimes acted as spies behind enemy lines. Others provided food, shelter, and logistical support for Union forces while risking their own safety. Their contributions were often overlooked for decades, but modern historians recognize these women as essential participants whose courage and dedication played an important role in shaping American history.

Did you know?

Harriet Tubman worked as a scout, nurse, and intelligence operative for Union forces, helping lead the 1863 Combahee River Raid in South Carolina, which liberated more than 700 enslaved people.
