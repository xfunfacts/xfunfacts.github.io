The Star-Spangled Banner Was Sewn in a Brewery xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] The original Star-Spangled Banner was sewn on the floor of a Baltimore brewery in 1813. Mary Pickersgill and her daughter created the enormous American flag.

The Star-Spangled Banner, one of America's most recognizable national symbols, was created under surprisingly humble conditions. In 1813, seamstress Mary Pickersgill, assisted by her daughter and several helpers, stitched the enormous flag on the floor of a Baltimore brewery because it was one of the few places large enough to accommodate its massive size. Measuring roughly 30 by 42 feet, the flag was commissioned to fly over Fort McHenry during the War of 1812. When British forces bombarded the fort, the flag remained standing, inspiring Francis Scott Key to write the poem that eventually became the United States national anthem. Today, the original Star-Spangled Banner is carefully preserved at the Smithsonian Institution, where it remains one of the country's most treasured historical artifacts. Its remarkable journey from a brewery floor to a national icon reflects both American craftsmanship and a defining moment in the nation's history.

Did you know?

Mary Pickersgill and her helpers made the large Fort McHenry garrison flag in 1813, using a nearby brewery's spacious floor to assemble its enormous fabric sections.
