Rabbits Can See Behind Themselves Without Turning Their Heads xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Rabbits can see behind themselves thanks to the wide placement of their eyes on the sides of their heads.

Rabbits have eyes positioned on the sides of their heads, giving them an exceptionally wide field of vision that allows them to see nearly all the way around their bodies without turning their heads. This adaptation helps them detect approaching predators while feeding in open areas. The trade-off is that rabbits have a small blind spot directly in front of their noses. Their wide-angle vision is one reason they react so quickly to sudden movement.

Did you know?

A rabbit's field of vision approaches 360 degrees, but it has a small blind spot directly in front of the nose and another behind the head.
