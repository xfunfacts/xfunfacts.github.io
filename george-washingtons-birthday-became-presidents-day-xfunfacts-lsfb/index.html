George Washington's Birthday Became Presidents' Day xFunFacts

The fact

[xFunFacts] Presidents' Day traces its roots to George Washington's birthday and celebrates the legacy of America's first president.

George Washington's birthday, observed on February 22, eventually became associated with the federal holiday now widely known as Presidents' Day. While the official federal holiday is still legally named Washington's Birthday, many Americans use the occasion to recognize the achievements of multiple U.S. presidents, especially Washington and Abraham Lincoln. Schools, museums, and historical sites often hold educational events highlighting the nation's early history and the development of the presidency. The holiday also reminds people of Washington's lasting influence on the nation's democratic traditions and constitutional government.

Did you know?

The federal holiday officially remains Washington's Birthday, observed on the third Monday in February; “Presidents' Day” is a popular name rather than its official federal designation.
